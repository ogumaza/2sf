2sf 0.3.0 adds the player: `2sfplay`, a foobar2000 component and an XMPlay plugin. They play every set that `2sfrip` makes, including the sets that vio2sf can't play. `2sfrip` now rips 35 games: the music of Pokémon from Diamond to White 2, Mario Kart DS, seventeen games on Procyon Studio's Digital Sound Elements (DSE), Super Princess Peach and more, with the games' streams.

The programs run on Windows 10 or later and require no runtime DLLs. For macOS, the README explains how to build the programs and the foobar2000 component from source.

## New

- **`2sfplay`** renders 2SF sets to WAV or FLAC files, for the length and fade of their tags. It boots the cartridge image of the set on a model of the DS with both CPUs and the sound unit, and the code of the game makes the sound. The output is 16-bit stereo at 32,728 Hz, the rate of the DS sound unit.
- **The foobar2000 component**, `foo_input_2sfplay`, plays `.mini2sf` and `.2sf` files on the same player. It shows the tags, edits them, and renders ahead for quick seeking. Advanced preferences › Decoding › 2SF decoder (2sfplay) sets the default length and fade, the curve of the fade, endless playback, rendering ahead and the settings of an album's track.
- **The XMPlay plugin**, `xmp-2sfplay`, does the same in XMPlay. Its options page is among the decoders in XMPlay's options.
- **Tracks play as on a soundtrack album.** `2sfplay`, the component and the plugin skip the silence before the first sound of a track and play 0.5 s of silence in its place. A loop plays the passes in its tags and fades over its tagged fade. 1 s of silence follows each track. Settings change each of these, and a loop with `loop_start` and `loop_length` tags can play another number of passes. `2sfplay --raw` plays a file as it is.
- **The fade** at the end of a track follows the square of the time left in it, as on most soundtrack albums. `--fade-exponent` in `2sfplay`, and a setting in the component and the plugin, change the curve from 0.5 to 4. 1 fades linearly.
- The component and the plugin hide the tags that set how a set plays in other players, such as `_arm9clk` and `_arm7clk`, and keep them when the other tags are edited. The player ignores them.
- **Games that start their music through a music manager.** Pokémon Diamond, Pearl and Platinum, HeartGold and SoulSilver, Black and White, Black 2 and White 2 (USA) and Mario Kart DS (USA) start each piece through routines of the game. The stub calls those routines, and the manager sets the channels of the tracks, the volume and the reverb as in the game. A piece that the game plays in another state gets a mini for each state: the field music of Black, White, Black 2 and White 2 in each state that their field units set, the TV music of Diamond, Pearl and Platinum in a room, the Wi-Fi game of Platinum at its fast tempo, and the music of each course of Mario Kart DS on the final lap, faster and a semitone higher. The sets contain the field music and the short pieces (MEs). `2sfrip` compares the start of each mini with the profile of the game and warns of any difference.
- **Patched game code.** Without a save, Pokémon Black, White, Black 2 and White 2 stop at an error screen that never returns, and their sets leave out the call of that screen. Mario Kart DS queues its title music while it boots, and its set leaves out that call. A set changes the code of a game only where the game can't reach its music otherwise.
- **Poké Transfer.** Pokémon Black, White, Black 2 and White 2 send a download-play program that plays two pieces of Pal Park. `2sfrip` rips the program as a second set beside the set of the game, with a library named after the library of the game with `_child_r_eng` added. `SEQ_BGM_PALPARK_GAME_HURRY` plays the minigame's piece as in the last 20 s of a round, 11 % faster and 1.33 semitones higher.
- **The title streams of Black 2 and White 2.** These games set up the stream player only on their title screens. Like the title screen, the mini of each title stream calls the stream manager of the game.
- **The Zelda games' states and effects.** Phantom Hourglass and Spirit Tracks start their music through a sound manager, and each piece plays in the state that the scene sets: Spirit Tracks gets 28 such states, and Phantom Hourglass two of the sea's music. The `_INTRO` minis of Spirit Tracks play the intro at full volume and then fade to the state of the mixer, in the frames in which the game does it. In the cutscenes that play a stream beside the music, the minis keep the same two channels free. `--effects` adds the sound effects of both games from their sequence archives, as minis titled `Effect` with the archive and the index, such as `Effect 0-2`.
- **Pokémon Mystery Dungeon: Blue Rescue Team** (USA). The set starts each piece with the routines of the game, and the tracks get the channels that the game gives them. The set contains the field music and the short pieces (MEs).
- **Fire Emblem: Shadow Dragon** (USA) and **Fire Emblem: Shin Monshou no Nazo: Hikari to Kage no Eiyuu** (Japan, revision 1). Both games keep their sound in two memory areas side by side, and `2sfrip` finds the one that contains the sound archive. The sets contain the sequences outside the sequence archives, with the six fanfares (FF_) that the games play for items, level-ups and wireless matches. Shin Monshou no Nazo makes room for its ending piece BGM_SYS_ENDING4 by starting its sound again with one larger memory area. The set does the same before that piece.
- **The Legend of Zelda: Four Swords Anniversary Edition** (USA and Europe), from images with the secure area decrypted. The game runs only on a DSi, and its sets run in the DSi mode of 2sf's player. The player picks that mode from the header of the image. The minis contain `_dsi=1`. The sets contain the 28 pieces of music in the sound archive and the 8 area themes that the game plays from files of its own with its player of streams.
- **Streams.** A set of a game on the SDK's sound archive player contains the streams of the archive, the recorded audio beside the sequences, and the code of the game plays them. Each stream file goes in a library of its own, and the mini of the stream names it as `_lib2`. Streams that share a record get one mini, as sequences do. Phantom Hourglass's sets gain 18 streams and Spirit Tracks' 21, and the sets of Shadow Dragon and Shin Monshou no Nazo contain 3 and 4. A stream mini fails the rip when the game changes the stream after its start. `--stream-files` writes the stream files of the game (`.strm`) beside the set instead. A `.strm` file doesn't contain the volume that the sound archive gives its stream.
- **Games on DSE.** `2sfrip` rips seventeen games that play their music with Procyon Studio's Digital Sound Elements (DSE): Pokémon Mystery Dungeon: Explorers of Sky, Explorers of Time and Explorers of Darkness (USA), Inazuma Eleven (Japan, revision 1), Inazuma Eleven 2: Kyoui no Shinryakusha: Fire (Japan, revision 1), Inazuma Eleven 3: Sekai e no Chousen!! Bomber and The Ogre (Japan), Professor Layton and the Diabolical Box, the Unwound Future and the Last Specter (USA), Fushigi no Dungeon: Fuurai no Shiren DS 2, 4 and 5 (Japan), Luminous Arc and Luminous Arc 2 (USA), Luminous Arc 3: Eyes (Japan) and Soma Bringer (Japan). The stub makes the calls with which the game starts a piece, and the code of the game loads and plays it. Each piece starts at the level at which the game starts it, and a piece that the game starts at a second level gets a second mini. Shiren 5 lowers three pieces in the frame in which it starts them, and their minis do the same. A song whose files and start volume repeat an earlier song gets no mini, and `2sfrip` names it in its output. The Explorers games end with `bgm0000`, a piece that none of their routines starts: the set changes the one instruction that refuses it.
- **London Life.** Professor Layton and the Last Specter contains a second game, London Life, with pieces of its own. `2sfrip` rips it as a second set beside the set of the game, with the library `NTR-BLFE_ll.2sflib`. The names of its pieces end in `_ll`.
- **The streams of the Professor Layton and Inazuma Eleven games.** These games play some of their music, many short pieces and jingles, and in the Professor Layton games a few sound effects as streams in Procyon Studio's sadl format. The sets contain them, each in a library of its own, and `2sfrip` decodes them as the stream player of each game does.
- **Super Princess Peach** (USA), on Nintendo SPD's sound driver. The set contains the 96 sounds of the music player that make sound, and two variants of 02_007 and 03_002 with the branch flag that sends them on to sections that they don't reach otherwise.
- **The backup memory.** `2sfrip` starts each game with no backup memory and with a blank one of each size, and the library keeps every file that any of those start-ups reads. Every mini runs on each start-up. A Naruto game from Japan (NTR-AREJ) shows an error screen without a backup memory and its logo screen with one. Its set now plays in a player that gives the game a backup memory.
- **DSi-enhanced games**, such as Pokémon Black, rip as a DS runs them.
- `--list` marks a song whose files repeat an earlier one, and the numbers that share a record.

## Changed

- **Length tags.** Every length now counts from the first sound, the first sample that isn't 0, where it counted from the start-up of the game. A loop plays two passes to the millisecond and fades over 7 seconds, where it was rounded to the second with a fade of 10 seconds, and gets `loop_start` and `loop_length`. A piece that ends gets its length at its last sound and a fade of 1 second. `2sfrip` takes the first sound, and the last sound of a piece that ends, from a render of each mini by 2sf's player, and prints both first sounds of each mini.
- **The length analysis plays what the set plays.** A piece that ends with the game's reverb running gets its last sound with the echo included. Music that has no loop or end within 600 s is measured again up to an hour.
- **Tags.** `2sfrip` no longer writes the game, the year or the copyright of Beat City, Phantom Hourglass and Spirit Tracks from tables in its code. The game tag is the name of the image file unless `--game` sets it, and `--year` and `--copyright` set the others. Beat City's sets list the songs by number, music first: song 105, the music of the title screen, is now "Song 105", like the other songs, and no longer comes first. The number in the file name of each mini changes with it.
- **Numbers that share a record.** A sound archive can give several sequence numbers the same record: the same sequence, bank, volume, priorities and player. They play the same in a set. Only the first now gets a mini, and `2sfrip` names the others in its output and in `--list`. Phantom Hourglass's set has 112 minis of sequences where 0.2.0 made 212, and Spirit Tracks' 228 where it made 226, with the states above. Some minis get new numbers in their file names.
- **The libraries.** The library of a game on the SDK's player keeps each file of the cut sound archive at its place in its card page, and keeps every file that the full image reads before the hook. Every start-up of the library must read the same files as the full image, with the same count of card reads and at the same time. Some libraries gain files.
- **Messages for damaged input.** An image whose secure area is encrypted now gets the message about the secure area first, also when its unit code isn't the code of a DS, DSi-enhanced or DSi game.
- **File names.** A title whose part before the first dot is a Windows device name, such as `CON`, gets a `_` in the names of its mini, its library and its files (`CON_`), and invalid UTF-8 in a title becomes `_`. No known game has such a title. When one run rips two images into one folder, the second rip no longer writes over the minis of the first. A mini whose name is taken gets `_2`. `!playlist.m3u` lists only the last rip's minis.

## Players

Every set that 2sf makes plays in 2sf's player. Beat City's sets also play in vio2sf 0.26.2. These sets don't play in it:

- The sets of Phantom Hourglass and Spirit Tracks stay silent: vio2sf can't run the start-up of these games. 2sf doesn't change the start-up of a game to suit one player. These sets play only in 2sf's player. The same is true of any other game whose start-up vio2sf can't run.
- Four Swords Anniversary Edition runs only on a DSi, and vio2sf has no DSi mode. Its sets also play only in 2sf's player.
- The stream minis of the Professor Layton and Inazuma Eleven games end at once with no sound in vio2sf 0.26.2 (32-bit foobar2000 on Windows).

Soma Bringer's sets play in 2sf's player. They haven't been tried in vio2sf 0.26.2.

vio2sf and 2sf's component both open `.mini2sf` files. If both are installed, foobar2000 uses one of them for these files, and it may use vio2sf. To play the sets of Phantom Hourglass, Spirit Tracks and Four Swords Anniversary Edition and the stream minis of the Professor Layton and Inazuma Eleven games in foobar2000, remove vio2sf (`foo_input_vio2sf`) in Preferences › Components and install 2sf's component. Do the same for any other set that stays silent in vio2sf. 2sf's component plays Beat City's sets as well.

## Known limitations

- The player saves no states. In a track longer than the render-ahead buffer of 10 minutes and 10 seconds, or in endless playback, a seek back before the buffered audio renders the track again from its start.
- `2sfrip` rips only the SDK's streams that a game starts one at a time, the streams of Four Swords Anniversary Edition and the sadl streams of the Professor Layton and Inazuma Eleven games. Streams in other formats, and songs that chain several streams, aren't ripped.
- A mini of an SDK stream plays its stream once, at the volume that the sound archive gives it. Where the code of a game loops a stream that ends, or lowers the volume of a stream, the mini doesn't.
- Sequences in sequence archives, where most sound effects are, aren't ripped, except the sound effects of Phantom Hourglass and Spirit Tracks with `--effects`. `--effects` doesn't rip the Pokémon games from Diamond to SoulSilver yet.
- The sound effects of the games on DSE and of Super Princess Peach aren't ripped, apart from a few that the Professor Layton games play as streams.
